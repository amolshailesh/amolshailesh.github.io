About

I work at the point where field research meets policy. For most of the past decade that work has been in rural India: first with grassroots organisations in Maharashtra's underserved districts during two severe droughts; then embedded inside the Government of Maharashtra's Department of Rural Development; and most recently on a randomised controlled trial in Bihar on women's collective action and access to MGNREGA, the world's largest public workfare programme.

My training is quantitative. I completed an MSc in Social Research Methods at LSE as a Chevening Scholar, following an MA in Development Economics, and I work mainly in Stata, with R and Python alongside. Recently I have been doing more qualitative and mixed-methods work: my MSc dissertation combined a telephonic survey and two embedded experiments with semi-structured interviews, and the interviews did most of the work of telling me what to measure.

I am currently based in Nashik and am looking for research roles in impact evaluation and policy research, with a view to doctoral study. I am happy to discuss collaborations on local governance and social protection in South Asia.

Research interests: social protection and public employment programmes; political reservation and local governance; caste and gender inequality in labour markets; measurement of discrimination; monitoring and evaluation systems in government.

Descriptive representation, substantive exclusion: elite backlash against Scheduled Caste village presidents in rural India

2025–26

India reserves a share of village council leadership for Scheduled Castes, but political quotas do not guarantee that elected leaders actually exercise authority. Existing work has documented elite capture in detail. This study asks what happens to the leaders who refuse to be a proxy for elites.

Question
How does the exercise of independent authority by Scheduled Caste village heads relate to the obstruction and exclusion they face from elites and officials?
Design
Telephonic survey of 299 serving village heads in Bihar (150 SC, 149 non-SC), drawn as a stratified random sample from the State Election Commission register; a paired-profile conjoint experiment and a cash-burning experiment embedded in the non-SC instrument; seven semi-structured interviews of SC leaders in Bihar and Maharashtra.
My role
Sole researcher. Designed both instruments and the two experiments, conducted the interviews, trained and supervised the survey firm's enumerators, and carried out all analysis in Stata.
Findings
Resistance is concentrated within and around the council rather than at the block office — false complaints, non-cooperation by elected colleagues, and interference by former incumbents each exceed reported bureaucratic obstruction. Just under two-thirds of SC village heads report at least one form of obstruction or exclusion in office; around one-fifth report five or more. Both experiments returned nulls, which I read as a limit of the measurement rather than evidence of no effect.
Output
MSc dissertation, Department of Methodology, LSE (Supervisor: Dr Saide Mobayed). A related article drawing on the pilot interviews appeared in the Graduate Inequality Review, University of Oxford.

Collective action and women's access to MGNREGA

2023–25

A randomised controlled trial in rural Bihar testing whether organising women workers into a collective improves their access to public employment and increases their labour market participation.

Question
Does collective action by women workers improve access to MGNREGA entitlements and labour market participation?
Design
Cluster-randomised trial across 125 village councils, with phone and in-person survey rounds covering 6,250 households, alongside scraped and cleaned MGNREGA administrative records.
My role
Senior Research Associate, with Dr M. R. Sharan (University of Maryland) and Dr Niharika Singh (University of Notre Dame / LSE). Managed field implementation and the data pipeline: programmed SurveyCTO instruments, trained and monitored enumerators, ran high-frequency data quality checks, built the Stata cleaning and merging pipeline, and acted as the main link between the research team, the survey firm and the implementing partner.
Status
Ongoing. Analysis in progress.

Social audits and MGNREGA delivery in Andhra Pradesh

2022–23

Whether mandated social audits change what actually gets delivered under India's public employment guarantee, and whether they reduce leakage.

Question
What effect do social audits have on MGNREGA programme delivery and leakages?
Design
OLS regression with a five-year panel of administrative data.
My role
Sole author. Scraped, cleaned and analysed the administrative data.
Output
MA dissertation, South Asian University.
